Project Jaeger

Fall 2025 - present Python · Gymnasium · PyBullet · Stable-Baselines3 Ongoing

Jaeger is an attempt to get a six-degree-of-freedom biped to walk in a straight line, and to do it in a way that could eventually survive contact with real hardware. It's the project I've spent the most time on and the one where the most things have gone wrong.

The environment

I built the simulation myself rather than using an off-the-shelf locomotion benchmark, which meant writing the URDF model, setting joint limits, and tuning contact dynamics until foot-ground interaction stopped producing physically impossible behavior.

The most important constraint is that the control signals are clamped to ranges a real actuator could actually produce. This is what would be crucial for future real-world implementation. Constraining the action space early costs training performance and buys a chance at sim-to-real transfer later.

Control architecture

The policy doesn't drive the joints directly. Underneath it sits a physics-based PD controller that already knows roughly how to hold a pose, and the PPO policy outputs residual actions layered on top of it. These are corrections rather than commands.

This matters for two reasons. The controller gives the agent a sane starting point, so early training isn't spent rediscovering that legs should stay underneath the body. And because the learned component is only a correction term, the failure mode when the policy encounters something unfamiliar is degradation toward the PD controller's behavior rather than immediate collapse.

Training

Training uses PPO through Stable-Baselines3, with runs tracked in TensorBoard. Most of the iteration has been on shaping the reward function.

An agent will find every loophole a reward leaves open. If you were to reward forward velocity alone, it might learn to fall forward productively. If you penalize falling too hard, it may learn to stand still forever. Both of these were scenarios that I had to work through.

Where it stands

  • Custom Gymnasium environment with a URDF-modeled 6-DOF biped
  • Actuator-realistic action clamping in place from the start
  • PPO residual policy over a PD baseline, training stably
  • Straight-line walking; turning and uneven terrain are next

The eventual goal is to put a version of this on physical hardware, though that may have to wait until I settle down into a more permanent living situation before doing so.
